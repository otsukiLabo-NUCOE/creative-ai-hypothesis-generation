# Neuro-Symbolic Fine-Tuning of Large Language Models Using Formal Verification Traces

## Abstract  
This paper introduces a neuro-symbolic framework for mitigating factual hallucinations in large language models (LLMs) by integrating structured knowledge derived from formal verification traces. As LLMs increasingly operate in domains requiring rigorous logical and mathematical reasoning, their tendency to generate plausible but incorrect content — commonly referred to as hallucinations — remains a critical challenge. We propose a fine-tuning methodology that leverages knowledge graphs constructed from verified computational traces to guide model inference, thereby anchoring generation in logically sound representations. Our approach embeds symbolic constraints directly into the neural architecture, enabling the model to reference and validate intermediate steps against formal proofs. The goal is to achieve a statistically significant reduction in factual errors while maintaining high levels of fluency and coherence in output. By bridging the gap between sub-symbolic learning and symbolic reasoning, this work advances the development of more trustworthy AI systems capable of reliable performance in complex, multi-step domains such as automated theorem proving, formal specification, and verified software analysis.

## 1. Introduction  
Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in generating human-like text across diverse domains, yet their reliance on statistical patterns often leads to factual inaccuracies, particularly in tasks involving logical deduction, mathematical computation, or multi-step reasoning. These errors, known as hallucinations, manifest when models extrapolate beyond training data or conflate superficially similar concepts, resulting in outputs that are coherent yet incorrect. The challenge intensifies in domains where precision is paramount, such as formal verification, automated reasoning, and safety-critical applications. Existing mitigation strategies — including retrieval-augmented generation, post-hoc fact-checking, and rule-based constraints — offer partial solutions but typically lack seamless integration with the generative process, often compromising fluency or scalability. To address this gap, we propose a neuro-symbolic fine-tuning paradigm that embeds structured knowledge graphs derived from formal verification traces directly into the LLM’s inference pipeline. By grounding generation in logically verified representations, our approach enables the model to consult and adhere to verified facts during production, reducing the risk of erroneous outputs. This work introduces a novel alignment between symbolic reasoning — rooted in formal proofs — and sub-symbolic learning, aiming to produce AI systems that are not only fluent but also factually reliable in complex, reasoning-intensive contexts.

## 2. Related Work  
Research at the intersection of large language models and symbolic reasoning has increasingly focused on integrating structured knowledge to improve factual accuracy and logical consistency. Early approaches leveraged external knowledge bases to enrich model context, enabling more informed generation through retrieval-augmented frameworks. These methods improved factual grounding but often required explicit query reformulation or post-processing steps that disrupted natural flow. More recently, efforts have turned to embedding symbolic constraints directly into neural architectures, using rule-based systems or logic programs to guide inference during generation. Such neuro-symbolic architectures aim to blend the representational power of deep learning with the precision of formal logic, though implementation challenges persist in scaling to complex, multi-step reasoning tasks. Another emerging direction involves learning from verified computational traces — such as proof sequences or model-checking outcomes — to construct knowledge graphs that encode reliable relational structures. These graphs serve as interpretable representations that can be consulted during generation, offering a pathway to align statistical prediction with logical entailment. While prior work has demonstrated promise in controlled settings, few have systematically addressed the integration of formal verification-derived knowledge into end-to-end LLM fine-tuning pipelines. This paper builds on these foundations by proposing a unified framework that fuses structured knowledge from formal verification with neural adaptation, targeting the reduction of hallucinations in reasoning-intensive domains.

## 3. Proposed Method  

We introduce a neuro-symbolic fine-tuning framework that integrates structured knowledge graphs derived from formal verification traces into the inference pipeline of a large language model (LLM). The method operates in three stages: knowledge extraction, graph embedding, and symbolic-guided generation.  

First, formal verification traces — such as proof sequences from theorem provers or model-checking outcomes — are parsed to construct knowledge graphs (KGs). Each node represents a logical entity (e.g., variables, propositions, or program states), and edges encode verified relationships (e.g., entailment, equivalence, or temporal succession). This KG serves as a symbolic substrate of verified facts.  

Next, we embed this KG into the LLM’s latent space using a differentiable encoder that maps KG nodes and edges to continuous vector representations. Let **G** = (𝒩, **E**) denote the knowledge graph, where 𝒩 is the set of nodes and **E** the set of edges. For each edge *e* ∈ **E** connecting nodes *u* and *v*, we define a symbolic constraint function **φ**_e: ℝ^d × ℝ^d → {0, 1}, where φ_e(**h**_u, **h**_v) = 1 if and only if the corresponding logical relation holds in the verification trace. Here, **h**_u and **h**_v are the hidden embeddings of nodes *u* and *v*, respectively. During fine-tuning, the LLM minimizes a joint loss:  

**L** = **L**_CE + λ·∑_{e∈𝔈} (1 − φ_e(**h**_u, **h**_v))² + **L**_KL  

where **L**_CE is the cross-entropy loss for language modeling, **L**_KL encourages alignment between KG embeddings and pre-trained token distributions, and λ balances symbolic constraint adherence.  

Finally, during generation, the model consults the KG via a differentiable retrieval module that activates symbolic constraints as soft priors. When generating a token, the system evaluates the consistency of candidate tokens with active KG relations, suppressing outputs that violate verified logical conditions. This mechanism ensures that generated content remains aligned with formally validated reasoning paths, thereby reducing factual hallucinations while preserving fluency through continuous, context-aware refinement.

## 4. Experimental Design  

This section outlines a planned validation framework for the proposed neuro-symbolic fine-tuning method, designed to assess its effectiveness in reducing factual hallucinations while preserving fluency and coherence in reasoning-intensive domains. The design emphasizes controlled evaluation across multiple logical and mathematical tasks, with a focus on reproducibility and methodological rigor.  

The evaluation will center on three task categories: (1) automated theorem proving, (2) formal specification interpretation, and (3) verified program analysis. For each category, a suite of benchmark problems will be constructed using established formal verification tools — such as SMT solvers or model checkers — to generate both training traces and gold-standard test instances. Knowledge graphs will be extracted from verified traces during pre-training, and the fine-tuned model will be tested on out-of-distribution instances requiring multi-step logical inference.  

Performance will be measured using two complementary metrics: (a) factual accuracy, assessed by comparing model outputs against verified traces, and (b) fluency-coherence scores derived from human evaluation and automated readability metrics. A statistical analysis — including effect size estimation and confidence intervals — will determine whether the neuro-symbolic approach yields a significant reduction in errors relative to a baseline LLM fine-tuned without symbolic constraints.  

To ensure reproducibility, all code, knowledge graph schemas, and evaluation scripts will be open-sourced under a permissive license. The experimental pipeline will be containerized and documented in a detailed README, enabling independent replication. While this work remains in the design phase, the proposed protocol provides a clear, transparent roadmap for future validation, laying the groundwork for rigorous assessment of neuro-symbolic integration in language models.

## 5. Reproducibility  

To ensure full reproducibility, all components of the proposed framework will be made publicly available under an open-source license. The codebase will include modular implementations for knowledge graph extraction from formal verification traces, differentiable embedding layers, symbolic constraint enforcement, and the neuro-symbolic fine-tuning pipeline. Knowledge graph schemas will be standardized using schema.org and custom logical relations, with example instances derived from verified theorem-proving traces. All datasets — including training traces, test instances, and ground-truth verification outcomes — will be hosted in a version-controlled repository with clear data lineage documentation. Hyperparameters, random seeds, and hardware specifications (e.g., GPU model, memory configuration) will be explicitly logged in configuration files. The experimental pipeline will be containerized using Docker or Singularity, with a reproducible build process documented in a README. Dependencies will be managed via environment files (e.g., requirements.txt or pyproject.toml), and all evaluation scripts will include automated unit tests. This comprehensive documentation and infrastructure will enable independent researchers to replicate the methodology, validate results, and extend the work to new domains.

## 6. Discussion of Applications Across Multiple Domains  

The neuro-symbolic fine-tuning framework presented here has broad applicability across domains where logical rigor and factual accuracy are paramount. In software engineering, verified program analysis and automated bug detection can benefit from integrating formal verification traces into AI-assisted code review, reducing the risk of erroneous optimizations or security vulnerabilities. In artificial intelligence safety, the method supports the development of more reliable decision-making agents by anchoring reasoning to logically verified priors, particularly in high-stakes environments such as autonomous systems or medical diagnosis support.  

Legal and compliance domains also stand to gain, as the framework can be adapted to generate contextually accurate interpretations of regulatory text grounded in verified legal schemas. Scientific research, especially in formal methods and verification-assisted experimentation, could leverage the approach to automate the generation of hypothesis-driven explanations that align with mathematically validated models. Furthermore, educational platforms offering instruction in logic, mathematics, or formal verification could use the system to provide explanations that are both fluent and factually grounded.  

By bridging symbolic reasoning with neural generation, this work enables AI systems to operate with greater transparency and trustworthiness. Its modular design allows customization across domains, making it a scalable foundation for future systems that require both creativity and correctness.

## 7. Limitations  

While the proposed neuro-symbolic fine-tuning framework offers a promising approach to reducing factual hallucinations, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the reliance on formal verification traces constrains applicability to domains where verified logical or mathematical derivations are readily available, limiting scalability to open-ended or less formalized tasks. Second, the symbolic constraint enforcement mechanism may introduce brittleness, as overly rigid adherence to verified relations can suppress valid but novel interpretations, potentially reducing generative flexibility. Third, constructing high-quality knowledge graphs demands significant preprocessing effort, and errors in trace parsing or schema design propagate directly into model behavior. Fourth, the current formulation assumes perfect alignment between neural embeddings and symbolic rules, yet the differentiable nature of the encoder may not fully capture discrete logical semantics, leading to approximation errors. Finally, while fluency is preserved through continuous optimization, the trade-off between constraint adherence and natural language coherence remains an open research question. These challenges highlight the need for further refinement in symbolic integration, scalability, and robustness across diverse reasoning contexts.

## 8. Conclusion  

This paper presents a neuro-symbolic fine-tuning framework that integrates structured knowledge graphs derived from formal verification traces into large language models to mitigate factual hallucinations in reasoning-intensive domains. By embedding verified logical relations directly into the generative process, the approach bridges symbolic precision with neural fluency, offering a pathway to more trustworthy AI outputs. While the method shows clear theoretical advantages and a structured experimental design, its effectiveness depends on the availability of high-quality verification traces and careful constraint tuning. Future work should address scalability, constraint relaxation, and cross-domain adaptation. Nonetheless, this work advances the integration of formal methods into language models, establishing a foundation for AI systems that are not only fluent but also logically grounded.
