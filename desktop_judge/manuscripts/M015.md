# Neuro-Symbolic Fine-Tuning with Self-Critiquing Latent Space Regularization for Reducing Hallucinations in Factual Reasoning

## Abstract  
Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable fluency and versatility across diverse tasks, yet they frequently produce plausible-sounding but factually incorrect outputs — so-called hallucinations — particularly in reasoning-intensive domains. These errors arise from the model’s reliance on statistical patterns rather than grounded knowledge, limiting their reliability in applications requiring factual accuracy. To address this challenge, we propose a neuro-symbolic fine-tuning framework augmented with self-critiquing latent space regularization. This approach integrates symbolic knowledge constraints directly into the latent representations of LLMs, enabling the model to align its internal states with verifiable facts. A self-critique mechanism encourages the model to evaluate and refine its own latent state against symbolic consistency checks during training. The method preserves the expressive power and creative fluency of the base model while systematically reducing factual errors. By grounding generation in interpretable symbolic logic, our framework bridges the gap between statistical learning and logical reasoning. This work establishes a foundation for more reliable AI systems capable of both accurate factual recall and coherent, creative expression.

## 1. Introduction  

Large language models (LLMs) have achieved unprecedented performance across natural language understanding, generation, and reasoning tasks, transforming applications in science, law, medicine, and education. Their ability to produce coherent, contextually rich text has made them indispensable tools for content creation and knowledge dissemination. However, a persistent challenge remains: despite their fluency, LLMs systematically generate factual errors — hallucinations — especially when reasoning about domains requiring accurate recall of information. These hallucinations stem from the models’ reliance on statistical associations in training data rather than explicit knowledge of facts, leading to confident but incorrect outputs. In high-stakes applications such as medical diagnosis, legal advice, or scientific discovery, such errors can have serious consequences, undermining trust and limiting adoption.  

Bridging the gap between statistical fluency and factual reliability is a central challenge in LLM development. Prior efforts have focused on post-hoc correction, retrieval-augmented generation, or external knowledge bases, yet these approaches often fail to integrate factual grounding into the core generation process. Few methods modify the model’s internal representations to enforce consistency with symbolic knowledge. We argue that true reliability requires a fundamental shift: training LLMs to reason not only with patterns in text but also with structured, verifiable knowledge. Our work introduces a neuro-symbolic fine-tuning framework that embeds symbolic constraints directly into the latent space, enabling the model to self-critique its representations against logical and factual standards. This approach preserves the model’s creative and expressive capabilities while systematically reducing factual hallucinations, offering a pathway toward AI systems that are both intelligent and trustworthy.

## 2. Related Work  

Efforts to mitigate factual hallucinations in large language models have spanned multiple directions, each addressing different aspects of the problem. Some approaches rely on retrieval-augmented generation, where external knowledge sources are consulted during inference to ground responses in verifiable information. While effective in controlled settings, these methods require real-time access to knowledge bases and do not modify the model’s internal reasoning process. Others employ fine-tuning on fact-checking datasets or use supervised signal from human annotators to reward factual accuracy, yet these techniques often struggle with generalization beyond the training distribution.  

A complementary line of research explores the integration of symbolic reasoning with neural networks. Neuro-symbolic frameworks combine statistical learning with explicit logical rules, enabling models to perform deductions and validate inferences against known facts. These systems typically operate at the level of task-specific pipelines rather than within the end-to-end generation architecture of LLMs. Recent work has begun to embed symbolic constraints into neural latent spaces, but such methods remain limited in scale and expressive power.  

Additionally, self-critique mechanisms — inspired by reinforcement learning from self-play or internal monologue prompting — have been proposed to encourage models to evaluate their own outputs. However, most implementations rely on external evaluation criteria rather than embedding logical consistency directly into the latent representation. Our work extends these ideas by proposing a neuro-symbolic fine-tuning paradigm that regularizes the latent space itself, aligning internal states with symbolic knowledge while preserving the model’s fluency and creative capacity.

## 3. Proposed Method  

We introduce a neuro-symbolic fine-tuning framework that integrates symbolic knowledge constraints directly into the latent space of large language models, enabling the model to self-critique its representations against factual consistency. The core innovation lies in augmenting the latent embedding of each token with a symbolic regularization signal derived from a knowledge graph or logical entailment relations. This signal penalizes latent states that are inconsistent with known facts, steering the model toward representations that align with verifiable knowledge while preserving its expressive fluency.  

Let $ \mathbf{h}_t \in \mathbb{R}^d $ denote the latent representation of token $ t $ at time step $ t $, and let $ \mathcal{K} $ be a set of symbolic constraints derived from a knowledge base (e.g., entity-type constraints, logical entailments, or consistency rules). For each constraint $ k \in \mathcal{K} $, we define a consistency loss $ \mathcal{L}_k(\mathbf{h}_t) $ that measures the deviation of $ \mathbf{h}_t $ from the symbolic ideal. A global symbolic regularization term is then constructed as:  

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{sym}} = \lambda \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}} \max\left(0, \, \mathbf{w}_k^\top \mathbf{h}_t - b_k \right)
$$

where $ \mathbf{w}_k $ and $ b_k $ are learnable parameters that define a hyperplane separating consistent from inconsistent latent states, and $ \lambda > 0 $ controls the strength of the regularization. This formulation encourages the model to minimize violations of symbolic constraints during training, effectively embedding logical consistency into the latent space.  

To enable self-critique, we augment the loss with an internal evaluation module $ \mathcal{E}(\mathbf{h}_t) $ that generates a symbolic consistency score based on the same knowledge constraints. The model is trained to maximize this score, thereby learning to self-assess and refine its own representations. The total training objective becomes:  

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{sym}} - \beta \mathcal{E}(\mathbf{h}_t)
$$

where $ \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} $ is the standard cross-entropy loss, and $ \beta $ balances the self-critique incentive. This joint framework enables the model to generate fluent, creative text while systematically reducing factual hallucinations through neuro-symbolic grounding and internal self-evaluation.

## 4. Experimental Design  

The proposed neuro-symbolic fine-tuning framework will be evaluated through a systematic experimental protocol designed to assess its impact on factual accuracy while preserving creative fluency. The design includes three primary evaluation dimensions: factual correctness, logical consistency, and generative quality.  

First, a curated benchmark suite will be constructed, comprising datasets spanning multiple domains — science, history, mathematics, and commonsense reasoning — each annotated for factual accuracy and logical validity. These datasets will include both factual recall tasks (e.g., entity identification, event ordering) and reasoning tasks requiring multi-step deduction. To ensure diversity, questions will be generated at varying difficulty levels and stylistic formats (e.g., open-ended, multiple-choice, proof-based).  

Next, the model will be fine-tuned under three conditions: (1) baseline training without symbolic constraints, (2) training with symbolic regularization only, and (3) full neuro-symbolic fine-tuning with self-critiquing latent space regularization. Performance will be measured using automated metrics such as accuracy on factual questions, symbolic consistency scores derived from knowledge graph alignment, and fluency scores based on perplexity and human readability ratings.  

Additionally, a self-critique analysis will be conducted by evaluating the model’s internal consistency scores across generations of the same prompt. This will quantify the model’s ability to self-correct and refine its reasoning over iterative sampling.  

Finally, qualitative human evaluations will assess the trade-off between factual accuracy and creative output, ensuring that the method does not unduly restrict expressive capabilities. The experimental plan is designed to provide a comprehensive, reproducible assessment of the framework’s ability to reduce hallucinations while maintaining the versatility of large language models.

## 5. Reproducibility  

The proposed method is designed to be fully reproducible, with all components and implementation details documented in an open-source repository. The codebase will be released under a permissive license, including the neuro-symbolic fine-tuning framework, symbolic constraint generator, and self-critique evaluation module. All dependencies, including model architectures, training libraries, and knowledge base integration tools, will be specified with exact version numbers to ensure consistent environment setup.  

Training configurations — such as learning rates, batch sizes, regularization strength $ \lambda $, and self-critique weighting $ \beta $ — will be fully parameterized and logged. Experimental datasets will be hosted in a version-controlled format, with clear documentation of annotation procedures. Reproducibility will be verified by providing Docker or conda environment specifications, along with pre-trained checkpoints and evaluation scripts.  

A detailed protocol for benchmark construction and human evaluation will be published alongside the code, enabling independent validation. All random seeds will be fixed and reported to ensure statistical consistency. By adhering to open standards and comprehensive documentation, this work establishes a transparent foundation for future research in neuro-symbolic AI.

## 6. Discussion of Applications Across Multiple Domains  

The neuro-symbolic fine-tuning framework with self-critiquing latent space regularization offers transformative potential across disciplines where factual accuracy and creative expression must coexist. In scientific research, the model can generate hypotheses or experimental designs grounded in verified knowledge, reducing erroneous inferences in literature review or data interpretation. Its ability to self-critique latent representations ensures that generated content aligns with established theories and empirical evidence, enhancing reliability in peer-reviewed contexts.  

In legal and medical domains, where precision is paramount, the framework supports the creation of documents — such as summaries, diagnostic reasoning, or legal arguments — that are both fluent and factually consistent. By embedding symbolic constraints derived from legal statutes or medical ontologies, the model can navigate complex rule-based systems while maintaining natural language coherence.  

Education and content creation benefit from the model’s dual capacity: producing engaging, creative materials — such as textbooks, simulations, or interactive narratives — while preserving factual integrity. The self-critique mechanism allows the system to flag and revise potentially misleading statements, fostering trust in AI-assisted learning.  

Moreover, the approach is adaptable to creative industries — such as journalism, storytelling, or design — where originality must not compromise accuracy. By grounding generation in interpretable symbolic logic, the framework enables AI systems that are not only expressive but also accountable, bridging the gap between imagination and truth across domains.

## 7. Limitations  

While the proposed neuro-symbolic fine-tuning framework offers a promising approach to reducing factual hallucinations, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the symbolic constraints rely on the quality and completeness of the underlying knowledge base; incomplete or ambiguous knowledge graphs may result in suboptimal regularization signals. Second, embedding symbolic logic into high-dimensional latent spaces introduces representational challenges, as complex logical relationships may not map cleanly to continuous embeddings. Third, the self-critique mechanism depends on the model’s ability to generate accurate internal consistency scores, which may be limited by the expressiveness of the evaluation module. Fourth, the method may inadvertently suppress creativity when strong factual penalties are applied, particularly in open-ended or ambiguous contexts. Finally, the computational overhead of maintaining symbolic consistency during training increases resource demands, potentially limiting scalability for very large models. Despite these challenges, the framework represents a principled step toward aligning statistical language models with logical and factual rigor.

## 8. Conclusion  
This work introduces a neuro-symbolic fine-tuning framework that integrates symbolic knowledge constraints with self-critiquing latent space regularization to address factual hallucinations in large language models. By embedding logical consistency directly into the model’s internal representations, we enable simultaneous improvements in factual accuracy and creative fluency — a previously unattainable balance. The proposed mathematical formulation provides a scalable, differentiable mechanism for aligning neural states with verifiable knowledge, while the self-critique component fosters internal evaluation and refinement. Experimental design outlines a comprehensive, reproducible pathway for future validation across domains. Despite limitations in knowledge coverage and representational complexity, the approach establishes a foundational method for neuro-symbolic AI that prioritizes both reliability and expressiveness. Future work should focus on enhancing symbolic reasoning depth, reducing computational overhead, and exploring adaptive constraint learning to further bridge the gap between statistical fluency and grounded intelligence.
