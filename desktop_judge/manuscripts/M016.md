# Interpretable Neural Architecture Search for Generative Models via Reinforcement Learning

## Abstract  
Generative models, such as generative adversarial networks and transformers, excel at producing high-quality synthetic data but suffer from opacity, making their decision processes difficult to interpret and debug. This lack of transparency limits their deployment in safety-critical and high-stakes applications. We propose a novel framework that integrates neural architecture search (NAS) with reinforcement learning (RL), guided by interpretability constraints. Our method formulates architecture discovery as a sequential decision process where the RL agent maximizes a reward combining generation quality and local interpretability metrics, such as pathway sparsity and feature attribution consistency. By embedding interpretability directly into the search objective, the framework encourages the evolution of architectures with inherently explainable decision pathways. This approach bridges the gap between high-performance generative modeling and trustworthy AI, enabling users to debug, validate, and audit model behavior during and after training. The proposed method is domain-agnostic and applicable across modalities, offering a pathway to more reliable generative systems.

## 1. Introduction  
Modern generative models have achieved remarkable performance across domains such as image synthesis, natural language generation, and audio creation. However, their success is accompanied by a fundamental challenge: opacity. These models operate as complex, high-dimensional black boxes, making it difficult to understand why specific outputs are generated, how to debug erroneous behavior, or how to ensure alignment with human intent. This lack of interpretability undermines trust, hampers debugging, and restricts deployment in safety-critical domains such as healthcare, law, and autonomous systems.  

A promising avenue for improving model interpretability is Neural Architecture Search (NAS), which systematically explores architectural spaces to optimize performance. When combined with Reinforcement Learning (RL), NAS enables dynamic, data-driven discovery of architectures tailored to specific objectives. Yet prior work has largely focused on maximizing accuracy or efficiency, with interpretability treated as a secondary concern or post-hoc analysis.  

We address this gap by proposing a framework that embeds interpretability constraints directly into the NAS-RL loop. Our approach formulates architecture discovery as an RL problem where the reward function balances generation quality against local interpretability metrics, such as pathway sparsity and feature attribution stability. By incentivizing architectures with inherently explainable decision pathways, our method enables the automatic discovery of models that are both high-performing and locally interpretable. This work establishes a foundation for trustworthy generative AI, where performance and transparency are co-optimized from the design phase.

## 2. Related Work  

Efforts to improve the interpretability of deep neural networks have spanned multiple paradigms, including post-hoc explanation methods, inherently interpretable models, and interpretable architecture search. Post-hoc techniques such as gradient-based attribution, saliency maps, and attention visualization provide insights into model behavior but do not alter the underlying architecture, leaving the core decision process opaque. Inherently interpretable models, such as decision trees, rule-based systems, and linear models, offer transparency but often sacrifice the representational power of deep architectures, limiting their applicability to complex generative tasks.  

Neural Architecture Search (NAS) has emerged as a powerful tool for discovering high-performance architectures through automated exploration of design spaces. Recent advances integrate NAS with optimization techniques like reinforcement learning, evolutionary search, and Bayesian optimization, enabling scalable and data-driven architecture discovery. However, most NAS-RL approaches optimize for accuracy, efficiency, or training stability, with interpretability typically addressed as an external concern. A few works have begun to incorporate interpretability as a regularization term or constraint, often through architectural priors such as sparsity or modularity, but these remain heuristic and rarely embedded within the reward function.  

Our work extends this literature by proposing a framework that jointly optimizes generative performance and local interpretability using RL-guided NAS, establishing a principled approach to co-designing expressive and explainable architectures.

## 3. Proposed Method  

We introduce a Reinforcement Learning-guided Neural Architecture Search (RL-NAS) framework designed to discover generative model architectures that simultaneously maximize generation quality and ensure local interpretability. The core idea is to treat architecture discovery as a sequential decision process in which an RL agent proposes candidate architectures, trains them on a small validation set, and receives a reward that balances two competing objectives: generative performance and pathway interpretability.  

Let $ \mathcal{A} = \{A_1, A_2, \dots, A_T\} $ denote a sequence of proposed architectures over $ T $ episodes. For each episode $ t $, the RL agent selects an architecture $ A_t $ from a search space $ \mathcal{S} $, which includes variables such as layer types, connectivity patterns, and the presence of interpretable modules (e.g., sparse attention, gating mechanisms). After training $ A_t $ for a fixed number of steps, the agent evaluates two metrics:  

1. **Generation Quality** $ Q_t $: A scalar measure of performance, such as log-likelihood or FID score on a held-out validation set.  
2. **Local Interpretability Score** $ I_t $: A composite score derived from pathway sparsity $ s(A_t) $ and feature attribution consistency $ c(A_t) $. Specifically, $ I_t = \alpha \cdot s(A_t) + \beta \cdot c(A_t) $, where $ s(A_t) = 1 - \frac{\|\mathbf{W}\|_1}{\|\mathbf{W}\|_0} $ quantifies the proportion of zero weights (encouraging sparse, modular pathways), and $ c(A_t) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \text{sim}(\phi_i(A_t), \psi_i(A_t)) $ measures alignment between pre- and post-attention feature representations (encouraging stable, explainable decision paths).  

The RL reward function is defined as:  
$$
R_t = \gamma \cdot Q_t + (1 - \gamma) \cdot I_t,
$$  
where $ \gamma \in [0,1] $ controls the trade-off between performance and interpretability. The agent maximizes the expected cumulative reward $ \mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=1}^T \gamma^t R_t] $ using policy gradient methods.  

By embedding interpretability directly into the reward, the proposed method encourages the evolution of architectures with inherently explainable decision pathways, enabling the discovery of generative models that are both high-performing and locally interpretable.

## 4. Experimental Design  

The proposed framework will be validated through a structured experimental program designed to assess both generative performance and local interpretability across diverse modalities. The design consists of four phases: (1) **Architecture Search**, (2) **Ablation Studies**, (3) **Interpretability Analysis**, and (4) **Domain Generalization Assessment**.  

In Phase 1, the RL agent will search the architecture space using a policy optimized via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), with reward shaping based on the combined metric $ R_t = \gamma Q_t + (1 - \gamma) I_t $. Search will be conducted on synthetic datasets (e.g., variably complex 2D image generation tasks) to ensure reproducibility and controlled evaluation of pathway sparsity and feature attribution dynamics.  

Phase 2 will involve ablating key components of the interpretability reward: (a) removal of the sparsity term $ s(A_t) $, (b) removal of the consistency term $ c(A_t) $, and (c) varying the trade-off parameter $ \gamma $. This will quantify the contribution of each interpretability signal to overall reward maximization and architectural diversity.  

Phase 3 will conduct local interpretability analyses on selected architectures using gradient-based feature attribution and pathway tracing. Metrics such as attention sparsity maps, feature activation consistency, and human-in-the-loop validation will be employed to assess whether discovered pathways align with intuitive decision logic.  

Phase 4 will evaluate generalization across domains by applying trained interpretable architectures to novel data distributions (e.g., cross-dataset image generation or text-to-image transfer). This will test the robustness of both performance and interpretability under distributional shift.  

All experiments will be logged using standardized metrics and open-source tooling, ensuring transparency and reproducibility.

## 5. Reproducibility  

To ensure full reproducibility, all experimental components — including the RL policy code, architecture search environment, dataset generation scripts, training pipelines, and evaluation metrics — will be released under an open-source license on a public repository (e.g., GitHub). The codebase will be containerized using Docker to guarantee consistent software and hardware environments across systems. Hyperparameters, random seeds, and evaluation protocols will be logged in a central configuration file and version-controlled via Git. All synthetic datasets will be generated deterministically using fixed noise seeds and shared code, while evaluation datasets will be sourced from well-documented, publicly available benchmarks. Performance metrics will be computed using standardized libraries with pinned versions to prevent drift. Reproducibility checks will be performed by an independent third party, and results will be published in a formal technical report. This comprehensive approach ensures that the proposed framework can be independently validated, extended, and deployed by the broader research community.

## 6. Discussion of Applications Across Multiple Domains  

The proposed framework for interpretable generative model discovery holds transformative potential across diverse domains where performance and transparency are jointly critical. In healthcare, interpretable generative models can synthesize medical images or patient data while preserving explainable pathways, enabling clinicians to validate synthetic examples against real cases and debug model biases. In robotics and autonomous systems, interpretable architectures can generate control policies with traceable decision logic, enhancing safety verification and human oversight. For creative industries such as design and entertainment, the method enables artists and developers to generate novel content — whether images, music, or narrative structures — with mechanisms to audit and adjust generative choices. In education and accessibility, interpretable generative tools can produce adaptive learning materials with transparent reasoning, supporting personalized instruction and inclusive design. Additionally, regulatory and compliance contexts — such as finance, law, and public policy — benefit from generative systems whose outputs can be audited for fairness, accuracy, and accountability. By embedding interpretability at the architectural level, the framework enables trustworthy deployment of generative AI in high-stakes, cross-domain settings where both quality and explainability are non-negotiable.

## 7. Limitations  

While the proposed framework advances the integration of interpretability into generative model discovery, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the RL-based search process is computationally intensive and may require significant time and resources to converge, particularly in high-dimensional architecture spaces. Second, the interpretability metrics — pathway sparsity and feature attribution consistency — are qualitative in nature and may not fully capture all dimensions of human-understandable decision logic. Third, the method assumes that local interpretability at the pathway level translates to global explainability, which may not hold for complex, multi-layered generative processes. Additionally, the framework has not yet been tested on real-world, high-stakes datasets, limiting its empirical validation. Finally, the trade-off parameter $ \gamma $ requires careful tuning; an overemphasis on interpretability may degrade performance, while excessive focus on quality may reintroduce opacity.

## 8. Conclusion  

This work introduces a novel framework that integrates neural architecture search with reinforcement learning, guided by interpretable design principles. By embedding pathway sparsity and feature attribution consistency directly into the reward function, we enable the automatic discovery of generative architectures that balance high-quality output with local explainability. The proposed approach addresses a critical gap in trustworthy AI, where performance and transparency are often treated as competing objectives. While computational and methodological challenges remain, the framework establishes a principled foundation for interpretable generative modeling across modalities. Future work will focus on empirical validation, scalability, and domain-specific extensions to realize its full potential.
